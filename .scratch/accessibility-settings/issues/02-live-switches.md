# 02 Live switches for accessibility, speech, steering and the cues

Status: resolved (2026-10-20)
Type: task
Blocked by: 01

`.scratch/accessibility-settings/spec.md`, "Switches" and "Environment variables". The switches move from `config_from_environment` (`speech.cpp`) into the model and become changeable while the game runs.

## Machinery

- **Values.** `accessibility.on`, `speech.on` and `steering.on` (names the implementer's to settle) join the model as its first group, ahead of `cues`, all shown, default on.
- **Hierarchy.** Accessibility over Speech, Steering and Cues; Cues over each cue's switch. A higher switch overrides without changing the saved values below it.
- **Live.** Every reader of `a11y::Config` that copied it at startup reads the live value instead. The cue engine is always created; Cues off mutes it. Accessibility off silences the readers and scene speech, stops the cues and stops steering. Steering off mid-glide returns the stick to centre that frame (spec open question 6).
- **The last announcement.** Turning Accessibility or Speech off says "Accessibility off" or "Speech off" before going quiet (spec open question 4). Turning either back on announces the switch's new value, and the reader then announces the focused row (open question 5).
- **Overrides.** `MELEE_A11Y`, `MELEE_A11Y_STEER` and `MELEE_A11Y_CUES`, when set, override the file for the run in both directions and are never written; a later change through the model applies and saves. `MELEE_A11Y=1` forces accessibility on.
- **Online.** Nothing here changes game state; the gates on `pc_net_resim()` stay as they are.

## Done when

- Unit tests cover the hierarchy, a live change of each switch, the overrides never written, and the last announcement before silence.
- The drive scripts that set `MELEE_A11Y=0` with `MELEE_A11Y_STEER=1` behave as before. A drive run with steering off in the run's `a11y.ini` steers nothing on character select, and the D-pad reaches quick chat.
- The `verify` skill's quick, menus and online levels pass, and `python tools/check_style.py`.
- `docs/a11y/speech.md` and the `verify` skill describe the switches as settings with the environment as overrides.

## Comments

### 2026-10-20, implementation (agent)

Implemented; Accessibility, Speech, Steering and Cues are live settings, with the environment as per-run overrides.

- **Where things live.** `switches.hpp/.cpp`: `Switches` registers `accessibility.on`, `speech.on` and `steering.on`, shown, each in a group of its own ("Accessibility", "Speech", "Steering" head them in the file), before the cue system registers anything, so they are the first rows and the file's first groups. It works out a `SwitchState` (accessibility, speech, steering, cues) from the model and sets speech by it. `hooks.cpp` creates it beside `s_settings`, applies the overrides (`Switches::override_from`) before the load, and `apply_switches` hands steering to `CssReader::set_steering` and `SssReader::set_steering` and Accessibility's part of the cues to `CueSystem::set_allowed`. `config_from_environment` no longer decides any switch: `Config` holds the three overrides as `std::optional<bool>` and a `speech` start state.
- **The hierarchy.** A switch under its parent is `parent && own`, unless an override holds it: then its override, against the parent too. That keeps the agent runs as before: `MELEE_A11Y=0` with `MELEE_A11Y_STEER=1` steers, and with `MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1` the cues sound. Cues reaches the engine through the mix level, `CueSettings::set_allowed` (Accessibility on, or Cues overridden) and `cues.on`, so each cue's own switch stays below it in the volume chain as before.
- **Overrides in the model.** `ValueModel::override_value` holds the value the game uses and keeps the file's own in `NamedValue::saved`: `read_text` updates `saved` silently (logging `<key> = <v> in the file, held at <v> for this run`), and `to_text`, `edit_text` and appending missing keys write `saved`, so an override never reaches the file, not even when a hand edit is read or another line is saved. `ValueModel::set` lifts it and counts as a change when either the game's or the file's value differs, so it is saved. `AccessibilitySettings::override_value` logs `<key> = <v> for this run (<VAR>=<v>), not saved`. Empty variables count as unset.
- **The cue engine always opens**, so the cues register their values whatever the switches say, and `a11y.ini` and the shown list always hold them. An output that fails to open leaves the cues running unheard (`CueSystem::sounding`), and "Audio cues could not start." is said after the proof-of-life.
- **Startup.** Speech starts off (`Config::speech = false`) and is set by `Switches::start` once the file is read, before "Non-Visual Melee ready"; a player who saved Accessibility off hears nothing and Prism is never started (`Speech::set_on` starts the bridge the first time speech is on). An error found as the file was read is logged `(off)` and said again after the proof-of-life. The log's order at startup changed: the cue engine, the overrides, the file, the speech backend, `switches start: ...`, then the proof-of-life.
- **Open question 4: the last announcement.** `Switches::update` says "Speech off" or "Accessibility off" (interrupt) while speech is still on, then turns it off: Prism has the text, and nothing sent afterwards can cut it off, since nothing more is sent. It is named by Accessibility when that changed. Back on, speech turns on and says "Speech on" or "Accessibility on" first. The speech log goes on recording while speech is off, marked `(off)`, so drive runs check it as before.
- **Open question 5: back on mid-page.** A reader learns of a switch change by watching `Speech::enabled()`, nothing else: `PageReader::follow_focus` remembers whether speech was on, and when it comes back announces the known control again, queued behind the switch's words, on its next frame, when the page already shows the new value; that value is then remembered, so the change is not said a second time. Both RmlUi readers get it (headless test in `test_port_menu_speech.cpp`). The main menu tree says its screen afresh (`MenuSpeech::forget` from the hooks' listener). Character select, stage select, the Training menu and the LAN lobby say nothing until something changes; their state stays current while speech is off, since they keep following the screen.
- **Open question 6: steering off mid-glide.** `Steerer::switch_off` ends a glide under way where the cursor is (logged `glide to "<target>" ended after <n> frames: steering was switched off`), drops the stick it was asking for and starts the glide afresh, so the next pad the hook publishes carries no glide stick. The screen's speech then names what is under the hand if it is not the destination, as for any glide ended elsewhere.
- **Drivers.** `drive.py` sets `MELEE_A11Y_STEER=1` only when the environment does not set it, so `MELEE_A11Y_STEER=` leaves Steering to the run's `a11y.ini`. `drive_net.py --muted` keeps Accessibility on in both instances, as `drive.py --muted` does.
- **Tests.** New suite `switches` (`test_switches.cpp`, wired as `pc_a11y_init` wires them): the rows, speech waiting for the switches, the hierarchy with saved values untouched, a live change of each switch by menu and by hand edit, the last and first announcements, the overrides in both directions with `MELEE_A11Y=1` rescuing a file that turned Accessibility off, and a glide ended by Steering off with the stick back at rest. `accessibility_settings` gained an override that holds and is never written; `speech` a live switch; `port_menu_speech` speech back on mid-page; `match_cues` checks the spec's whole Rows table, the switches first.
- **Verification.** Build with the "Rollback sections" line; style; unit tests, all passing (`launcher_data` passed this time). A bounded `title` run with `MELEE_A11Y=0 MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1`. `run_level.py` with `MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1`: the seven match scripts, the menus and the online level, 31 of 31; then menus and online without it, 24 of 24. By hand: a `drive.py --muted` run with `steering.on = off` in its file steered nothing at D-pad presses on character select; a run driven from a Python script editing its `a11y.ini` turned Steering on (read when F1 opened the port menu) and the D-pad steered, then off during a glide to Pichu and F1: the glide ended after one frame, "steering was switched off", and "Player 1: closed" was said for where the hand stayed; `drive_net.py --muted` with `steering.on = off` showed "You: Hello" on player 1's screen after two presses of D-pad Up, the hand unmoved; and the launcher with Speech turned off and on by hand edits said "Speech off", logged the next focus `(off)`, then "Speech on" and the focused button again, queued.
- **Docs.** `docs/a11y/speech.md`: "The switches" and "The environment overrides the file". `docs/a11y/cues.md`: the engine always open, Cues and Accessibility muting it, the overrides in the file. The `verify` skill: the switches' log lines and overrides, `MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1` now unmuting rather than opening, `MELEE_A11Y_STEER=` and `drive_net.py --muted`, the `switches` suite. `CLAUDE.md`'s speech and cue engine lines and `CONTEXT.md`'s speech entry name the switches.
- **For issue 04.** The rows need nothing of their own for these switches: change them through `AccessibilitySettings::set`, and the listener does the rest, the last announcement included. The readers announce the focused row by themselves when speech comes back, so a menu turning Accessibility or Speech on should not say the new value itself; turning one off, whatever the reader says after `set` goes unheard, as it should. A row whose value an override holds shows the value the game uses (`NamedValue::value`); `NamedValue::overridden` says it is held, if the row wants to say so.
- **For issue 03.** Preview must play with the switches off: Accessibility off and Cues off both reach the engine as the mix level (`CueSettings::mix_level`), so a preview has to bypass the mix level's switch, not only the cue's.
- **Left open.** The switches' help texts are a first proposal for the play test, as is "Accessibility on" followed by the focused row, which in the menu repeats the switch's value. The native screens other than the main menu tree say nothing when speech comes back.
