# 07 The gap warning

Status: resolved (2026-10-14)
Type: task
Blocked by: 06

A held tone while you are in the air with nothing below you. Read the spec's "The gap warning" first, and `docs/a11y/gameplay-state.md` ("Is there ground below?", "Reading safely").

## Machinery

- **The snapshot** gains, for you in the air, the result of `mpCheckFloor` cast from just above your feet down to the bottom blast zone: a floor found or not, and its line. The cast runs in the tick hook, between ticks, where it leaves no trace; guard every line id against -1 before passing it on.
- **The driver**: on while you are in the air, in play, and the cast found nothing; off otherwise, after a hold of a few ticks so it does not flicker at a platform's edge. Silent while you are knocked out or between death and respawn.
- **The sound**: a generated held, centred tone, low and distinct from both position cues, at constant pitch and loudness, looped.
- **Settings** registered: on or off, volume, boost, and the hold in ticks.
- The game's own warning, sound 96, stays as it is.

## Done when

- Unit tests for the driver: ground, air with floor below, air with a platform below, air over a gap, the hold on leaving a gap, knocked out.
- A drive script on Final Destination and Battlefield walks off an edge and recovers, and jumps between Battlefield's platforms: the log shows the gap warning starting past the main stage's edge and stopping on return, and never over the platforms or the main floor.
- `docs/a11y/gameplay-state.md` closes its open question on raycasts for this use: the game's own cast, called in the tick hook.
- `python tools/check_style.py` passes; the `verify` skill and `docs/a11y/cues.md` are updated; `CONTEXT.md` gains "Gap warning".

## Comments

### 2026-10-14, implementation (agent)

Implemented; the gap warning hums in every match while you are in the air with no floor below you.

- **Where things live.** `game_access.c`: `match_floor_below` fills the snapshot's new `A11yMatchState.below` (`A11yFloorBelow`: found, line, y). `gap_warning.hpp/.cpp`: the driver, `over_gap` and `GapWarning`, whose only state is the hold's count. `match_cues.cpp`: the "Gap warning" group, the looped voice "gap" and its sound. No new hook: the tick hook's snapshot carries the cast.
- **The floor below, shaped for issue 09.** `below` is filled for you whenever you are in play: on the ground the floor under your feet (`coll_data.floor.index`, read only while on the ground), in the air the cast's line and the floor's height straight below. Every line id is checked against the line count before it goes in, and `line` is -1 whenever `found` is false, outside a match included. So the ledge warning takes `below.line` on the ground and in the air alike, without reshaping. The gap driver reads only the air case.
- **The cast starts at the collision box, not the feet: a deviation from the spec's "from just above your feet".** The first drive run heard the warning come on for a few ticks as Link landed back on the main floor: in the air the feet sink up to about 6 units below a floor while the fighter's collision box (the ECB, which the game lands on) is still above it, so a cast from just above the feet started under the floor and met nothing. The cast now starts 1 unit above the box's bottom (`cur_pos.y + coll_data.ecb.bottom.y`). Recorded in `gameplay-state.md`, "Is there ground below?".
- **The raycast question is closed** in `gameplay-state.md` ("Is there ground below?", and gone from its open questions): the fork calls the game's own `mpCheckFloor` from the tick hook. What it writes, checked in `mplib.c` and `mpcoll.c`: its output parameters, and the joints' too-far marks with the `didCheckBounding` flag, which it sets and clears again within the call when the flag is clear on entry. Every user in the game pairs them the same way (the fighters' collision step per step, the ledge searches, the stage load), so between ticks nothing is marked and the cast leaves the collision data as it found it. Everything else it calls reads. The online runs, whose match entrances are in the air and so cast every tick, showed no desync.
- **The hold** is 10 ticks by default (a sixth of a second), 0 to 60, `gap_warning.hold`. It counts every tick of the hook, slow motion's skipped ticks included, so it is real time. Silence for being out, paused, before GO or outside a match is at once and resets the hold.
- **The sound, and why.** 120 Hz with its third and fifth harmonics at a third and a fifth of its level (the start of a square wave), looped from a 4800-sample buffer of whole periods, so the loop has no seam (a test renders it and checks). It is the only held cue, so its timbre already parts it from both pulses, and its register is below both: the opponent's 400 Hz pulse runs 200 to 800 Hz on the default mapping (overtone to 1600 Hz), your 1500 Hz tick 750 to 3000 Hz, and the hum's fundamental is 120 Hz. The odd harmonics, at 360 and 600 Hz, are there so laptop speakers and small headphones still play it, and give it a hollow, hum-like colour rather than a pure sine's, which reads less like an alarm. They do sit inside the opponent's range, so with the opponent low only the held-versus-pulsed difference keeps the two apart.
- **Level.** Boost 1, the sound peaking at 0.6 of full scale (0.5 RMS). In the dump of `match_gap.drive` the hum measures about -23 dB over a tenth of a second, the opponent's pulse windows about -21 and your tick's about -29: a little under the opponent's pulse and well over your tick, since a low tone sounds quieter than a higher one at the same level. A first try at half that level (-29 dB) sat level with your tick and was raised.
- **Tests.** `gap_warning` (new suite, `test_gap_warning.cpp`): ground, air with the main floor below, air with a platform below, a gap, the hold on leaving a gap (and a gap within the hold, and no hold), knocked out at once and on the respawn platform, paused, before GO, outside a match, slow motion. `match_cues` gains the group and its values in order after the position cues, the start line, the hold and the stop through the engine, and the looped tone rendered centred with no seam.
- **The drive scripts.** `tools/a11y/match_gap.drive` (Final Destination: a jump in place, then off the left edge and back with the midair jump) and `tools/a11y/match_gap_battlefield.drive` (Battlefield: main floor to the left platform, the top one and the right one, a drop through, then off the right edge and back), both in the debug VS match, two scripts since the debug match takes its stage from the environment. Each pause on a Battlefield platform logs Link's place through your cue's stop line (`pitch 1.15` on a side platform, `1.32` on the top), which the script waits for, so a missed jump fails it. The log shows one gap start a unit past the floor's end (-86.6 on Final Destination, 69.4 on Battlefield) and one stop a sixth of a second after Link is back over the floor. A drive script cannot fail on a line too many, so `run_level.py` gained `COUNT`, lines a run must log exactly so many times: the two gap lines once each.
- **Other scripts.** `match_position.drive` now logs a gap start and stop as Pichu runs off the edge, and `results.drive` a start as Link walks off Battlefield and its stop at his knockout; the `verify` skill says so.
- **Measured on the way:** the respawn platform is in the air with the floor below, so no warning there; hanging on a ledge is in the air with nothing below (Link at x = -88, y = -18.8 off Final Destination's left ledge), so the hum sounds through a hang. Left as the spec defines the gap, a question for the play test.

The pause trick in `match_gap_battlefield.drive` is a cheap way for a script to check where the fighter stands.
