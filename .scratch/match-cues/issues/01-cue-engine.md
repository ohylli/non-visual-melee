# 01 The cue engine

Status: resolved (2026-10-13)
Type: task

The engine every cue plays through, before any cue exists. Read the spec's "The cue engine", "The volume chain" and "The log, the dump and the trace" first, and ADR-0005. The throwaway prototype on the local branch `prototype/audio-cues` (`cue_prototype.cpp`, `a11y.cmake`) shows the SDL stream, the miniaudio download and the threading; reuse what fits, keep none of its demo.

## Machinery

- **miniaudio 0.11.25**, downloaded at configure with its pinned SHA256 as the prototype does, compiled once in its own small library target in `a11y.cmake`. Its compile definitions (`MA_NO_DEVICE_IO`, `MA_NO_ENCODING`) are the target's public usage requirements, so every fork file that includes the header gets the same struct layout; the prototype set them file by file. Its own warnings off.
- **The engine module**: opens a second SDL audio stream on the default playback device (32-bit float stereo, 48 kHz) whose callback renders the cue mix. The render step is a plain function over a buffer, so tests drive it with no device.
- **Voices**: created with a mono sound buffer and a mode, looped or pulsed. Per-tick targets (pan, pitch, gain, interval) written by the game thread; the audio thread glides toward them over a few milliseconds. Start and stop through the fork's own short gain ramp. Pulses retriggered on the audio thread, each one interval after the last at the interval current then. Constant-power pan; pitch by playback rate.
- **Sound buffers**: a type the generators of later issues fill, checked against the fastest interval a pulsed voice allows: a pulse that does not fit is refused at voice creation with a log line.
- **The cue mix**: fixed headroom and a soft limit at the output.
- **Volume chain**: master × cue volume × per-cue volume × per-cue boost × voice gain. Cue volume and per-cue values are plain engine inputs here, set to 1; issue 02 feeds them from cue settings.
- **Master volume hook**: `pc_a11y_master_volume(float)`, one line in `pc_audio_set_volume` (`src/pc/audio.c`), declared in `a11y_hooks.h`. It stores the value even before the engine opens.
- **Lifecycle**: opened in `pc_a11y_init`, closed in `pc_a11y_shutdown`; all voices stop with a ramp on `pc_a11y_scene_entered`. Failure to open logs why, speaks "Audio cues could not start." and leaves the game running without cues.
- **Switch**: `MELEE_A11Y_CUES`, in `config_from_environment` beside steering: on when `MELEE_A11Y` is on, and `MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1` turns it on with speech off. Off means the engine never opens.
- **Logging**: an `[a11y] cue engine` line at open with the SDL driver, device rate and buffer size, as the prototype logs; each voice start and stop as `[a11y] cue <name> start|stop` with its targets.
- **Dump and trace**: `MELEE_A11Y_CUE_DUMP=<file>` writes the mix as raw f32 stereo 48 kHz from the audio thread; `MELEE_A11Y_CUE_TRACE=<file>` writes every voice's targets each tick, as a text line per voice.
- **Analysis script** under `tools/a11y/`: reads a dump and reports, per window, level, left-right balance, pulse onsets and their intervals, and dominant pitch.

## Done when

- `cmake --build build` passes, with the "Rollback sections" line.
- Unit tests render the engine into a buffer and cover: constant-power pan keeps loudness across pan; starts and stops have no sample-to-sample jump above a threshold; a pulsed voice's onsets are its interval apart and an interval change applies from the next pulse; the volume chain multiplies; five voices at full gain stay under full scale; a pulse longer than its fastest interval is refused.
- A test writes a dump of a known pulse train, and the analysis script reports its intervals and balance correctly.
- A bounded run (`MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1`, dummy audio driver) logs the engine's open line and shuts down cleanly.
- `python tools/check_style.py` passes; the footprint grep shows the new hook.
- The `verify` skill gains `MELEE_A11Y_CUES`, the dump, the trace, the script and the expected open line.
- `docs/a11y/cues.md` opens with the engine's mental model (two streams, voices with targets, pulses on the audio clock, the volume chain) and how to check a dump.

## Comments

### 2026-10-13, implementation (agent)

Implemented; no cue uses the engine yet (issue 03 adds the first).

- **Where things live.** `cue_engine.hpp/.cpp`: the mix, voices, ramps, glides, pulses, volume chain, soft limit, log lines, dump and trace, with no device. `cue_output.hpp/.cpp`: the SDL stream, whose callback renders the engine in chunks of 256 frames. `cue_sounds.hpp/.cpp`: `read_sound` (any miniaudio data source into a `Sound`), `shape_edges` (raised-cosine ends) and `lead_in` (silence before the sound). `hooks.cpp` owns the engine and its output (`s_cues`, `s_cue_output`) and the master volume hook. `Config` gained `cues`, `cue_dump` and `cue_trace`.
- **The API, for 02 and 03.** `int add_voice(VoiceSpec)` takes a name, a cue number (0 to 15, whose level the voice takes), a `Sound`, a mode (looped or pulsed), the fastest interval and the lowest pitch; it returns -1 with a log line when refused. `start(voice, VoiceTargets)`, `set_targets`, `stop`, `stop_all`, `playing`. `VoiceTargets` is pan (-1 to 1), pitch (playback rate), gain (0 to 1), interval (seconds). Inputs of the volume chain: `set_master_volume`, `set_mix_level(CueLevel)` for the whole mix (all-cues switch, cue volume) and `set_cue_level(cue, CueLevel)` for each cue (switch, volume, boost); `CueLevel` is `{on, volume, boost}`, and off glides to silence with the voices still running underneath, so a pause fade (issue 03) can be the mix level's switch rather than stops and starts. `trace_tick(tick)` writes the trace's lines; the match tick hook should call it once a tick after setting targets.
- **The engine renders the voices itself**, not through `ma_sound`: per-sample glides of pan, gain and pitch, pulses placed to the frame, and the ramps need it, as the Star Fox 64 fork's backend found. Pitch is playback rate with 4-point Hermite interpolation. miniaudio is built (`a11y_miniaudio`, its definitions public, warnings off) and used for sounds: `read_sound` takes its waveforms and noise now and a decoder later. ADR-0005's "filled by miniaudio" is that division; the ADR stays as accepted.
- **Constants**, in `cue_engine.cpp`: ramp 5 ms (straight); pan and gain glide over about 10 ms, pitch over 20 ms (the Star Fox 64 fork's value); headroom 0.5 then a tanh soft limit above 0.6, so one full-scale voice stays in the straight part and five reach about 0.998. Highest pitch 4, longest interval 30 s, most boost 8.
- **Pulse timing.** A pulse fires when the time since the last one reaches the interval current at that moment, so an interval change made between pulses already counts for the gap under way; one shorter than the time already waited fires at once and the rhythm starts afresh from there. The fit check uses the lowest pitch: a sound plays longest pitched down, so `seconds / lowest_pitch` must not exceed the fastest interval. Every target is held to the voice's limits.
- **Start from silence** snaps the glides to the targets and pulses at once; a start while playing changes only the targets. A stop and start before the ramp ends ramps back up without restarting the sound. Only state changes are logged: `[a11y] cue <name> start|stop pan .. pitch .. gain .. [interval ..]`.
- **Trace format**: a header line, then per voice per tick `tick<TAB>voice<TAB>on|off<TAB>pan<TAB>pitch<TAB>gain<TAB>interval`, `-` for a looped voice's interval. Voices that are off are traced too.
- **The switch** goes beyond the spec: `MELEE_A11Y_CUES=0` also turns cues off with speech on; unset follows `MELEE_A11Y`; `1` turns them on with speech off.
- **Stop on scene entry** runs before the rollback gate: a scene change is never a re-run frame, and stopping is silent anyway.
- **Analysis script** (`tools/a11y/cue_dump.py`) uses the standard library only: the Python CMake finds for ctest (3.12 here) has no numpy. Onsets by a level threshold after 5 ms of quiet, pitch by the median spacing of upward zero crossings of the mono mix, pan by inverting the constant-power law. `test_cue_dump.py` checks it against `cue_engine_test`'s pulse train: onsets within 2 ms, pan within 0.05, intervals within 1 ms, pitch within 5 Hz. It reports pan +0.50 and -0.50 (-0.48 in the window where the pan glides), intervals 0.250 and 0.125, pitch 1000 Hz.
- **Under SDL's dummy driver** the device runs at 44100 Hz with a 1024-frame buffer and still pulls the mix in real time, so agent runs record true dumps: a 300-frame title run's dump held about 6.7 s. Opening took about 2 ms.
- **Verification**, online level, with `MELEE_A11Y_CUES=1` exported so the engine was open in every scripted run: build with the "Rollback sections" line; style; unit tests (`cue_engine` and `cue_dump_analysis` new; `launcher_data` fails as known); a mutation check (no ramp) fails the jump test; bounded `title` runs with cues on (open line, dump and trace lines, clean exit), cues off (no cue lines) and an unknown audio driver (`cue engine could not start: SDL audio did not start: Audio target 'nosuchdriver' not available`, "Audio cues could not start." queued, the game runs on); the sixteen menu drive scripts; the five `css_net.drive` runs and `lan_lobby.drive`, no desync. All passed, some only on rerun after the flake below.
- **The archive flake was a base port bug, which the engine made likelier; fixed in its own commit** (`file cache: store its own host buffers without the ARAM test`). With the engine open, the base port's known flake struck far more often: an archive garbled at a scene load (`ARCHIVE LbMcGame.: implausible header`, also `TyDatai.usd` and `LbRf.dat`, then a PANIC in `lbarchive.c`, exit 0xC0000409, or a freeze). It hit about 11 of 80 runs with cues on and none of about 70 with cues off, 3 of 30 against 0 of 30 in alternating pairs of `main_menu.drive`.
  - **Diagnosis.** Instrumentation compared the game's buffer, the cache entry and a fresh disc read at a garbled load. The buffer matched the cache entry, and the entry differed from the disc in 16557 of 19347 bytes, so the entry was wrong from the moment it was stored. Every garbled entry had been stored by the prewarm worker with `[from ARAM]`. `preload_single_file` reads into a `malloc`'d buffer and stored it through `pc_file_cache_put`, which takes any address below 0x01000000 for an ARAM offset. When the heap gave the buffer such an address, the cache copied ARAM instead of the file.
  - **How the engine came into it.** Starting SDL audio and its stream in `pc_a11y_init` changes the heap's layout, and low addresses came more often on the title path. They also come without the engine: parallel bounded `vs` runs got a low buffer in 6 of 36 runs with cues off as with cues on. So the bug reached players without cues too, more rarely.
  - **Evidence that the fix works.** Cues on with prewarm off gave 0 garbles in 30 runs (one input freeze). After the fix, 36 sequential cue-on runs of `main_menu.drive` passed (3 to 4 failures expected before), and two of them stored a low buffer correctly.
  - **No regression test.** No unit test seam reaches the bug: it needs `preload_single_file`'s disc read and a heap address below 16 MB, and neither has a fake. The check is the log: a store from a host buffer must never say `[from ARAM]` (in the `verify` skill).
  - **What the freezes were.** The two freezes after a scene load, and one more seen while diagnosing, fit the same cause (a garbled archive that loops instead of panicking); none recurred after the fix. gdb is not installed in MSYS2 here, so no thread stacks were taken.
- **Separate base port crash, not chased.** Six bounded `classic` runs at once (`MELEE_BOOT_SCENE=classic`) sometimes crash with an access violation reading 0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF in `lb_00B0.c`, right after `LbBf.dat` comes from the cache. It hit 2 of 36 runs with cues off and 3 of 36 with cues on before the fix, and 1 of 36 and 0 of 36 after. No `[from ARAM]` store precedes it, so the cue engine plays no part. All six instances of one parallel round also stalled together on `Surface present failed`, a display event.
